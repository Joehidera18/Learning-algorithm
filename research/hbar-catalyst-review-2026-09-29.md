# HBAR: turning a missed move into better evidence

Reviewed September 29, 2026 UTC. This note responds to the owner's report of
selling HBAR before its September 28 move. It is not a reconstructed trade or
an assertion that a strategy would have made money.

## What was publicly knowable

[The Hashgraph Group's September 23 release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/thg-and-ibm-sign-global-partnership-to-bring-idtrust-as-agentic-ai-identity-solution-on-ibm-cloud-catalog-as-know-your-agent-kya-becomes-an-enterprise-priority-302887805.html)
was published at 10:02 a.m. Eastern, 8:02 a.m. America/Boise. It described a
global IBM agreement and IDTrust's IBM Cloud Catalog availability. That is
earlier than the move the owner described. The release identifies a plausible
commercial channel; it does not quantify a resulting HBAR buy requirement or
guarantee token-holder returns.

The [IBM catalog entry](https://cloud.ibm.com/catalog/content/thg-idtrust-648d7223-f5c2-4ac4-82c8-1fda7687b2b1-global)
provides a second primary place to check the product. A press-release date is
not necessarily the first date a product existed or was listed. Syndicated
copies of the announcement are not separate commercial wins.

We have not established a complete timestamped price/order-book replay,
announcement-to-price causal link, or evidence that the running website saw
the release before the move. The private app's watch status and historical
discovery logs were not available in this work. The new reader cannot recover
an observation that an earlier installation never recorded.

## Changes motivated by the gap

- Keep HBAR and other recently sold assets in the research universe. A sale is
  not a reason to stop checking the original thesis.
- Maintain primary project/company feeds and source-health records alongside
  price observations. A broken source must remain visible, not count as silence.
- Keep the publisher's time separate from the app's first observation. Label
  initial snapshots, revisions, reprints, late discovery and outages.
- Put scheduled events in a sourced calendar. A scheduled upgrade or a clinical
  target is separate from its eventual outcome and from investment returns.
- Preserve negative evidence: security failures, safety concerns, financing,
  dilution, token supply, delays and unquantified commercial economics.
- Hand the actual saved evidence to AI research before forming a conclusion.
  Price thresholds describe observed activity; they do not forecast a rally.

The concrete implementation is [Market Radar](../MARKET_RADAR.md). Its direct
HBAR sources still do not cover every partner release. The Yahoo/CoinDesk feeds
are partial, and the calendar is manually maintained. In-app observations also
do not reach a sleeping or absent user through phone push in this release.

## How to evaluate whether the change helps

Before adding any event strategy to stock backtests, collect a prospective
journal with source failures, false positives and quiet periods as well as
successful-looking cases. Define entry/exit rules, realistic costs, holding
windows and the evaluation period before reviewing outcomes. Use the later of
publisher availability and app observation as the earliest information time.

Compare detection delay and coverage first. Then compare a fixed event rule
against a price-only rule and a simple benchmark on genuinely later data.
Keep all tested variants in the record and measure drawdown, turnover and
performance after costs. Do not backfill today's discoveries into yesterday's
decision time. This evaluation has not been run, and no win rate, profit
probability or promise to catch every move follows from the software tests.
