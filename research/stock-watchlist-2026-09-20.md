# 20 stocks positioned for emerging market needs

Research date: September 20, 2026. Market-price references: September 18, 2026 regular-session close. Primary investment horizon: 12–24 months; selected clinical-stage biotech and quantum programs require 3–5 years or longer.

My strongest research priorities are **Vertex, Alnylam, Broadcom, Alphabet, TSMC, and Eaton**. Among companies whose value depends heavily on an emerging breakthrough, **Beam Therapeutics** is my most interesting biotech candidate and **IonQ** my preferred speculative quantum candidate among those examined. Confidence in those two share-price outcomes is substantially lower.

I have a positive investment thesis for every company below, but these are not 20 equally attractive purchases at today's prices. The ranking reflects my judgment about evidence, business strength, financing, valuation, and relevant catalysts. It is not a modeled probability of a gain or a prediction that every stock will rise.

A useful distinction for your roughly one-year horizon: a company can approach a valuable clinical or technical milestone within a year while remaining several years from substantial related profits. Stocks can reprice on those milestones, in either direction.

## Where I think demand is heading

**1. Computing investment is creating demand for the equipment around the chips.** My inference is that power delivery, cooling, networking, manufacturing, and testing remain attractive because they solve specific constraints customers must address to deploy more computing. The IEA's April 2025 base case projected global data-center electricity consumption at about 945 TWh in 2030, more than double its then-current level. That is a scenario with uncertainty, not a guarantee of orders for any individual company. [IEA: Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary)

Recent company reports provide more immediate evidence: Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue, Arista's networking sales, and Eaton's electrical orders have expanded substantially. My investment test is whether this spending continues to produce earnings and cash, and whether customers earn enough from AI to sustain it. Several stocks on this list would suffer together if large cloud companies cut spending. [Broadcom Q3 results](https://investors.broadcom.com/node/64671/pdf), [Arista Q2 results](https://www.arista.com/en/company/news/press-release/24401-pr-20260804), [Eaton Q2 results](https://www.eaton.com/us/en-us/company/news-insights/news-releases/2026/eaton-reports-record-second-quarter-2026-results.html)

**2. Genetic and immune-system medicines offer increasingly concrete opportunities.** The most investable progress combines a defined biological target, meaningful human results, feasible delivery, and a route to reimbursement. Alnylam has substantial RNA-interference product sales; argenx has an established autoimmune franchise and a recent Phase 3 result; Beam has encouraging early human data. Those represent different levels of evidence and commercial maturity. A promising change in a blood biomarker is not automatically proof of improved long-term health. [Alnylam Q2 results](https://investors.alnylam.com/press-release?id=29986), [argenx ALKIVIA results](https://argenx.com/news/2026/press-release-3345813.html), [Beam September clinical update](https://investors.beamtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/beam-therapeutics-presents-updated-clinical-data-phase-12-trial)

**3. Obesity treatment needs can create room for better delivery and tolerability.** My thesis for Viking depends on useful differentiation, access, manufacturing, and economics. A large disease market alone does not establish that another drug will win enough patients at an attractive price. Its Phase 3 development makes the opportunity more concrete, but clinical success and commercial competitiveness remain unproven. [Viking Q2 update](https://ir.vikingtherapeutics.com/2026-07-29-Viking-Therapeutics-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results-and-Provides-Corporate-Update)

**4. Quantum computing is advancing, but broad profitability remains a longer-horizon thesis.** Google's research now includes neutral atoms alongside superconducting hardware. IBM has reported selected computational demonstrations, while IonQ is developing new manufacturing and control technology. These developments deserve attention; they do not establish that quantum computing will soon replace ordinary computers or GPUs. For a 12-month investment, Alphabet's and IBM's existing businesses matter far more to my judgment than hypothetical quantum revenue. [Google research expansion](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/neutral-atom-quantum-computers/), [IBM research assessment](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/quantum-advantage), [IonQ Superion announcement](https://www.ionq.com/news/ionq-launches-superion-product-line-industry-leading-upgradeable-platform-designed-to-scale-manufacturable-fault-tolerant-quantum-computing)

**5. Financing and valuation still matter in an innovation boom.** The Federal Reserve raised its policy-rate range to 3.75%–4.00% on September 16, citing elevated inflation. My implication for this list is to favor companies able to fund their plans and to demand more compensation for financing risk. A successful technology can still produce a disappointing stock return if investors initially pay too much or future share issuance absorbs much of the value created. [Federal Reserve statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)

## The individual investment cases

### 1. Vertex Pharmaceuticals — VRTX

**Evidence.** Q2 revenue reached about $3.33 billion. The existing cystic-fibrosis business remains dominant, while JOURNAVX and CASGEVY provide newer commercial opportunities. Vertex reported an FDA target decision date of **November 30, 2026** for povetacicept in IgA nephropathy. A scheduled decision is not an approval. Its August revenue outlook excluded the then-pending Crinetics acquisition; Vertex subsequently announced completion in September. [Q2 results and pipeline](https://investors.vrtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/vertex-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results), [Vertex investor announcements](https://investors.vrtx.com/)

**My thesis.** This is my first biotech research priority because a substantial operating business supports several distinct ways to create value. I do not need to assume every pipeline program succeeds. Kidney disease and pain could make the company less dependent on cystic fibrosis if clinical and commercial execution support expansion.

**Next test and valuation.** Watch the kidney decision, paid uptake of newer products, and updated acquisition economics. I would not value the combined company using pre-acquisition cash or guidance without adjustment. My thesis weakens if new launches disappoint persistently, acquisition spending fails to earn an adequate return, or the core franchise deteriorates. A single approval would not settle those questions.

### 2. Alnylam Pharmaceuticals — ALNY

**Evidence.** AMVUTTRA generated about $1.01 billion in Q2 product sales. Nevertheless, management reduced its 2026 TTR-franchise guidance to $4.2–$4.5 billion as second-line US cardiomyopathy demand normalized after the initial launch surge. Cash and marketable securities were approximately $3.3 billion at June 30. [Q2 release](https://investors.alnylam.com/press-release?id=29986)

**My thesis.** RNA interference is commercially established here, giving the platform more credibility than a purely preclinical investment. I see a plausible path to higher earnings if the treated population grows and development produces additional useful medicines.

**Next test and valuation.** The next two sales reports matter more to me than a large long-term market estimate. The preliminary equity valuation is around 5.8 times the midpoint of current total-revenue guidance, including collaboration revenue. That is not enterprise value or a profit multiple. Another meaningful guidance cut would weaken my confidence. Competition, reimbursement, financing obligations, and dependence on AMVUTTRA also constrain the upside case.

### 3. Broadcom — AVGO

**Evidence.** Q3 fiscal 2026 revenue was about $29.6 billion; AI semiconductor revenue reached $16.7 billion. Free cash flow was approximately $13.7 billion, and management guided the following quarter's total revenue to $34.8 billion. These are reported results and a forecast, respectively. [Q3 earnings release](https://investors.broadcom.com/node/64671/pdf)

**My thesis.** Custom accelerators and networking give Broadcom a credible route to benefit as large computing customers pursue performance, power efficiency, and control of their costs. I particularly value the evidence that demand is producing cash.

**Next test and valuation.** I want successive generations of customer programs to translate into durable revenue, with cash conversion holding up. The stock is already a very large company, so “AI will grow” is insufficient by itself. Customer concentration, customers designing more internally, manufacturing constraints, acquisition debt, and a slowdown in capital spending are meaningful risks. I would reduce conviction if growth increasingly relied on a narrowing customer base without corresponding returns on investment.

**Evidence.** Q2 revenue rose 24% to $119.8 billion. Headline profit also benefited from a very large investment gain, so a simple trailing price-to-earnings ratio can misrepresent recurring profitability. Separately, Google expanded its quantum research to neutral-atom systems alongside superconducting hardware. [AP earnings report](https://apnews.com/article/f914606d842d4c6848019083d667fc3a), [Google quantum research update](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/neutral-atom-quantum-computers/)

**My thesis.** Alphabet gives me a way to own a substantial existing business with exposure to AI infrastructure and a longer-term quantum opportunity. The quantum program is additional upside; I do not assign it a material contribution to next year's earnings.

**Next test and valuation.** I would evaluate recurring operating earnings, cash remaining after infrastructure spending, cloud economics, and whether new AI usage monetizes. I would remove exceptional investment gains before deciding the shares look inexpensive. ### 5. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing — TSM

**Evidence.** TSMC reported Q2 US-dollar revenue of $40.2 billion and guided Q3 to $44.6–$45.8 billion. Its manufacturing business is benefiting from demand for advanced semiconductor capacity. [Q2 results and outlook](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/quarterly-results/2026/q2)

**My thesis.** I like exposure to the manufacturing stage because competing chip designers can each create demand for sophisticated fabrication. The upside case does not require me to identify only one successful AI-chip architecture. Execution, yields, reliable delivery, and returns on expansion remain central.

**Next test and valuation.** Watch whether advanced capacity generates durable earnings after its enormous capital requirements. Valuation comparisons must account correctly for the relationship between US depositary shares and local shares, as well as currency; I have not used inconsistent vendor market-cap ratios here. Taiwan-related geopolitical risk is a major part of the investment case. Export restrictions, customer concentration, and expensive overseas expansion also matter. My thesis weakens if capacity additions outrun demand or pricing and utilization deteriorate together.

## What “on the edge of a breakthrough” should mean

For biotech, I would distinguish four questions:

1. Does the medicine engage its biological target?
2. Does that lead to meaningful patient benefit, with an acceptable safety profile?
3. Can regulators approve the intended treatment and population?
4. Can the company manufacture, deliver, and sell it economically?

Beam's early protein measurements, argenx's randomized trial result, and Alnylam's product sales answer different parts of that sequence. They should not receive the same probability of commercial success. This is why my speculative excitement is greatest around Beam while my stronger biotech investment confidence sits with Vertex and Alnylam.

For quantum computing, a **physical qubit** is a hardware element. A **logical qubit** is information protected through an error-correction scheme using physical resources. A machine with a larger raw qubit count is not automatically more useful. Error rates, usable circuit depth, connectivity, speed, error-correction overhead, and the best classical alternative matter. Selected demonstrations can be impressive without establishing broad, economical advantage. [IBM's discussion of current demonstrations](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/quantum-advantage)
